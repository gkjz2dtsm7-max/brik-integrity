Deathstock is not a ticker. It is investor and trade-press slang for the small basket of public death-care equities. Funeral Director Daily Death Care Index is one share each of Service Corporation International (SCI), Carriage Services (CSV), Matthews International (MATW, caskets and memorials), and Security National Financial (SNFCA). A September 3, 2026 note lists those four by name and puts 10-year price appreciation at SCI +209.9%, SNFCA +168.1%, CSV +44.8%, and MATW -65.4%. The same basket is what older coverage called death stocks. A separate retail meaning, dead stock, is unsold inventory and is not what these tickers are.

The Google Finance link is Security National Financial Corporation, NASDAQ: SNFCA, Salt Lake City. Carriage Services, Inc. is the Houston name behind carageservicesinc, NYSE: CSV. Neither runs a corporate PAC or a material federal lobbying program. The verified political money is small, personal or company-labeled at the state level, and sits well below the industry vehicles (SCI PAC, the National Funeral Directors Association PAC, and the International Cemetery, Cremation and Funeral Association PAC).

1. THE TWO COMPANIES

Security National Financial (SNFCA) was built around Security National Life, founded in 1965, and became a holding company in 1979. It runs three segments: life insurance, including preneed funeral plans sold in about 42 states; cemetery and mortuary (11 mortuaries and 5 cemeteries in Utah, 1 cemetery in California, 1 cemetery and 4 mortuaries in New Mexico); and mortgages. Headquarters is 433 West Ascension Way, Salt Lake City. Scott M. Quist is chairman, president, and CEO (CEO since 2012, director since 1986). Sons Adam G. Quist and Scott Andrew Quist are vice presidents and directors. Recent market cap is roughly $210 million to $236 million. It is family-controlled through Class C voting shares. It is in the death-care index because it owns funeral homes and cemeteries and sells funeral insurance, not because it is a pure funeral operator.

Carriage Services (CSV) is a pure death-care operator, founded in 1991, IPO in 1996, based at 3040 Post Oak Boulevard, Houston. Two segments: funeral home operations and cemetery operations. At December 31, 2025 it reported 155 funeral homes in 24 states and 28 cemeteries in 9 states. Carlos R. Quezada has been vice chair and CEO since June 2023. He was a managing director at Service Corporation International from 2009 to 2020. Founder Melvin C. Payne moved to executive chairman in that 2023 handoff. Recent market cap is about $500 million.

2. WHAT THE DONOR DATABASES ACTUALLY SHOW

Federal law bars corporate treasury checks to federal candidates. A company trail at the federal level has to be a connected PAC or itemized gifts from executives and employees. Utah allows corporate contributions, which is why a company name can appear on a state report.

No corporate PAC, either company. Searches of OpenSecrets organization profiles and FEC committee records did not turn up a Security National Financial PAC or a Carriage Services PAC. Do not confuse Security National with Security Finance of Spartanburg, which has its own OpenSecrets profile, or with the Eureka, California Security National / Rob Arkley real-estate operation. Do not confuse Carriage Services with Carriage House Partners LLC, a separate Texas legislative donor.

No material federal lobbying client file under either corporate name. The death-care lobbying that does show up is association money, not these two treasuries. OpenSecrets lists the National Funeral Directors Association as a lobbying client (about $100,000 in a recent cycle, $200,000 in some 2024 industry tallies) and the International Cemetery, Cremation and Funeral Association at about $40,000. The policy prize is the FTC Funeral Rule.

SNFCA, state, company-name hit. OpenSecrets 2021 contributor table for Utah Governor Spencer J. Cox (R) lists Security National Financial Corporation at $5,000, tied around rank 31. Cox reported total that cycle was $824,920. OpenSecrets caveat: depending on state law, the organization is not necessarily the donor, and the money often comes from affiliated individuals or PACs. The label on the report is the company name. That is the cleanest corporate-name political hit for SNFCA.

SNFCA, federal, CEO personal gifts. Campaignmoney FEC rollup for Scott Quist, Sandy, Utah 84092, employer Security National Financial Corp / President:
1. $2,700 to Friends of Mia Love (R) on March 16, 2017
2. $2,700 to Friends of Chaffetz (R) on March 6, 2017
3. 2016 cycle: 1 transaction, $2,500
4. 2000 cycle: 3 transactions, $2,000
5. 2018 cycle total on that page: 2 transactions, $5,400
6. No 2020-cycle itemized total on that rollup
A live FEC employer search for Security National Financial returns 49 itemized receipts. Visible rows are small employee-level gifts (Kellen Ashe to Harris for President and ActBlue in 2024, small WinRed and Trump receipts), not an executive program. Quist older trade role is real but not a PAC: president of the National Alliance of Life Companies from 1990 to 2000, treasurer and director into 2013.

Carriage, the one named PAC link. OpenSecrets 2023-2024 donor list for the ICCFA PAC (FEC C00385195) includes Robbie Pape, Houston, Texas 77027, occupation CARRIAGE SERVICES, $1,000 on May 19, 2023. The same page lists two later Houston Pape gifts of $1,000 each (April 13, 2024 and October 17, 2024) with occupation EXECUTIVE, same Houston ZIP cluster. The May 2023 row is the hard employer match. The later two are the same name and city, occupation not restated as Carriage. Pape is a Carriage regional executive. That is an individual gift into an industry PAC, not a company treasury check.
ICCFA PAC scale is small. In the OpenSecrets misc-services PAC table for 2023-2024 it gave $8,500 to federal candidates, all Republican. NFDA PAC (C00204008) raised $109,775 and gave $58,500 to candidates that cycle, 7.69 percent Democrat and 92.31 percent Republican. SCI PAC, the industry leader and a peer rather than a parent of either target, gave $28,800 to candidates in that same table.

Carriage charity gift, not campaign money. In 2025 Carriage pledged $100,000 to the Funeral Service Foundation, taking cumulative giving past $250,000. That is an industry nonprofit, presented at the NFDA convention. It is not a political contribution.

Carriage employee FEC noise. An employer search for Carriage Services returns small recurring ActBlue and WinRed gifts ($5 to $50 range) from individuals. Those are not executive-scale and are not a company program. No itemized federal program turned up under Carlos Quezada or Mel Payne tied to the Carriage employer field.

3. INDUSTRY LAYER, FOR SCALE

OpenSecrets Funeral Services (code G5400), 2024 cycle: $984,068 total. Of that, $851,128 from individuals, $95,800 from PACs, $37,140 soft or outside. To Democrats $335,825 (35.56%), to Republicans $605,521 (64.12%). Prior cycles sit in a similar band, usually about 60 to 68 percent Republican since 2012, with a 2020 spike to $2.08 million.
The money that actually moves in this industry is association and SCI money, not SNFCA or CSV treasuries. Older National Institute on Money in Politics work (From Coffins to Coffers) put death-care state giving above $6 million from 1999 to 2006, mostly state funeral-director associations and SCI, slight Republican lean, heavy to incumbents. That pattern still fits: SCI PAC, NFDA PAC, ICCFA PAC, and state associations are the vehicles. These two issuers are not.

4. WHAT IS NOT A TIE

1. No super PAC, 527, or dark-money vehicle found under either corporate name.
2. No federal contract trail of political size. Carriage has a small VA mortuary-services award history with deobligations, not a donor relationship.
3. Security Finance (Spartanburg), Eureka Security National, Carriage House Partners LLC, and Montana Rob Quist are different people and entities.
4. SCI is the consolidator and the political spender in this sector. Quezada prior SCI employment is a career fact, not an ownership or PAC link between SCI and Carriage.

5. BOTTOM LINE

The political money trail on these two death-care names is thin and mostly personal.
1. SNFCA: a $5,000 company-name label on Spencer Cox 2021 Utah report, plus Scott Quist itemized federal gifts to Mia Love and Jason Chaffetz in 2017 and smaller earlier Utah Republican gifts. No PAC, no federal lobbying file.
2. Carriage Services: Robbie Pape $1,000 ICCFA PAC gift in May 2023, with two later same-name Houston $1,000 PAC gifts whose occupation line says executive rather than Carriage. No PAC, no federal lobbying file. The $100,000 Funeral Service Foundation pledge is charity, not campaign money.
3. The real death-care political money sits with SCI PAC, NFDA PAC ($58,500 to candidates in 2023-2024, 92 percent Republican), and ICCFA PAC ($8,500, all Republican), inside an industry that gave about $984,000 federally in 2024, about 64 percent Republican.
Gaps that a paid OpenSecrets bulk download or a full FEC employer export would still close: every sub-$200 unitemized gift, the occupation line on Pape 2024 ICCFA rows, and whether the Cox $5,000 was a corporate check or an affiliated individual coded to the company. On the public record as it stands, neither company is a political spender.
